Section 02. What is broken

Ordered by where in the funnel each problem bites.

2.1  The audience does not exist as an artefact

This is the most serious problem in the programme. The HubSpot list used to promote webinars cannot be found, and neither of the two people who own webinar email can say who receives a webinar invite. The only recovery handle is a post event send on 31 July to about 1,700 contacts, still visible in HubSpot, whose composition is unknown.

This is the second motion orphaned by one departure inside three days. The first was the past attendee invite, which was a manual habit rather than a workflow and stopped when the person who did it left.

Consequence. The 2 September Truflation promotion has no defined audience. Everything downstream of a wrong list is wasted.

2.2  Registration friction

  • An apply to attend gate against a cold list of 1,800 names failed. Traction only came once the session was opened up through organic social.
  • The workstream owner has asked for the flow to be simplified to the smallest possible number of places and steps.
  • Registrations may have been collected across up to three paths on one recent webinar. Two are reportedly dead and it has never been verified where that registrant list actually lives.
  • Signups are the stated pain across the team.

2.3  Promotion runway is half what it should be

One to two weeks is the house standard. Three or more weeks with multiple touches is what the programme needs, and it is what the team has asked for on the next webinar.

2.4  The platform could not measure the thing it was bought for

LinkedIn Live reports registrants but not attendees, whatever third party streaming tool is used. The representatives did not disclose that before the contract, and the internal requirement had been stated up front. Attendance was visible only by monitoring the comments, and the workaround was screenshotting them. Identification ran at roughly 40 percent of registrants.

This is now resolved by decision. LinkedIn Live is out. The leads it produced, however, are still the leads the organisation is judging the channel on.

2.5  Production quality has failed in front of clients

  • A platform failure logged participants off immediately before a session. A debrief was called for and never assigned.
  • A live session opened with roughly two minutes of silence, with no holding slide and no hold music, in front of senior internal attendees.
  • A recording opened with a minute of unaired behind the scenes conversation.
  • Every published asset queues behind one editor for 3 to 4 days, after a legal step with no known turnaround.

WHY THIS IS NOW FIRST  These are the failures that moved production quality ahead of the value layer in Section 05. No amount of downstream content repairs a webinar that starts badly in front of a client.

2.6  Lead quality is why the channel is on trial

A recent webinar lead list was rejected outright as poor quality, with two usable names out of the whole session. The reading in the room was that the wrong people were attending from organic promotion.

The paid half of the explanation is already in hand. A quarterly advertising review shows three developer retargeting advertisements taking about 30 percent of impressions on about 10 percent of spend at a $0.68 CPM, against roughly $8 to $28 for video in the same quarter. A sixteen times gap inside one account is a placement signature, not a content one. The most reached companies rank by headcount rather than intent: roughly 67,000 employees at one bank at a frequency of 6.5, and five of the top thirty accounts are Indian retail banks. There is no seniority or job function filter.

THE DIAGNOSIS  LinkedIn Live could not tell us who attended, and LinkedIn paid was buying the wrong people cheaply. The webinar did not fail. The targeting did.

Two caveats travel with those figures. There is no meeting record for the advertising review, so it should be written up. And the source carries its own warning that row alignment in the lead column is ambiguous, so quote the CPM and the headcount skew but not the per row lead counts.

2.7  The intake list is out of date

Webinar requests are described as coming from three named people. One of them is currently absent, another marketing manager is co running the next webinar, and a third function controls promotion for the one after it. A process defined by three names breaks at the next departure. Intake should be defined by function.

2.8  The funnel has never been measured

Five separate meetings across six days decided a channel's value with no measurement in any of them. The only webinar figure spoken aloud in any of the five was 1,700, once, and the person who said it did not know its composition.

The channel was put on trial in one meeting on the basis that nobody appeared to be listening live or watching recordings, the verdict was parked for a later session, and at that session it was never raised. The channel was acquitted from the other direction, with no figure produced on either side.

2.9  A qualified lead cannot reliably reach a salesperson

The Salesforce to HubSpot direction is fixed. The HubSpot to Salesforce direction, which is the direction a new registrant travels, still refuses 70,031 records against a validation rule requiring a website, a primary means of contact, and a contact handle. Nobody owns it.

There is also a conceptual gap. The organisation has never elevated a lead that was not an explicit hand raiser, and a webinar attendee is not a hand raiser.

2.10  The bottleneck is sales capacity, not lead volume

  • A salesperson could not meet all the leads a previous campaign produced.
  • Replies from another campaign were reportedly ignored.
  • The team preference is a small number of well chosen names rather than twenty.

A programme that delivers two hundred qualified leads into a team that cannot work twenty fails visibly.

2.11  Governance and reporting

  • A live webinar ran and the director did not know it had happened.
  • A date, time and duration changed without the workstream owner in the decision, and the rework was absorbed silently. Landing page, creative, mailing list, calendar invite and duration all changed.
  • Recurring invites are stale. A colleague who left months ago is still on the webinar sync.
  • Two gating decisions collide and the collision has not been named out loud. See 5.6.

2.12  The dependency on announcements

Webinar topics today are largely derived from product announcements. The internal view is that announcements no longer move the market as they once did, and that there is less pipeline in the second half of the year and therefore fewer deals to announce.

If topics are sourced from announcements and there are fewer announcements, the pipeline dries up unless the programme learns to source its own topics. That is the strongest argument for the evergreen layer in Section 03.

Section 03. The marketing we are not doing

This is the value checklist. Every item here is approved in principle and none of it is built.

3.1  Micro content, one recording into many assets

Today the recording is cut once, uploaded once, emailed once, and then it stops working. The workstream owner has been trying to start clipping for months and has never had the capacity.

Asset from one 45 minute sessionCountDestination
Short clips, 30 to 90 seconds, captioned6 to 10 realisticLinkedIn, X, the library page
Pull quote cards5 to 8Social, speaker kits
One page summary1The gated asset bundle
Transcript1Search and the bundle
Newsletter items2 to 3The internal newsletters
Sales enablement snippet1Inbound representatives and sales

THE HONEST CONSTRAINT  At 3 to 4 days per edit through one editor, during two major events, thirty clips per webinar is not deliverable in September. Phase it, and do not commit a number before the capacity conversation.

3.2  The past attendee engine

Everyone who has ever registered for a Chainlink webinar should be invited to the next one automatically. Today this was one person's manual habit and it stopped when they left. A workflow does not resign.

3.3  The nurture sequence

There is one post event email carrying a recording link, and then nothing. No sequence, no branching, no next action. Vidyard already reports full watch behaviour to the contact record, so a sequence can branch on whether someone attended live, watched the replay, or did neither. That capability was bought in 2023 and is not being used.

What it should be: replay, then a one page summary, then a related asset, then a soft meeting offer.

3.4  The webinar library page

The request is for every webinar to be listed in chronological order with the recording playable on the page, with Vidyard remaining as the player. The current page is dead weight. Filters and categories have been added to the brief.

The value beyond the request is a search surface, a link sales can use, a destination for every clip, and the natural home for the asset bundle.

SCOPE CHANGE  The library page now sits inside a full website rebuild, which changes both its timing and who controls it. Do not let the replay destination wait for the rebuild. An interim page carrying the Vidyard embeds is worth having by October.

3.5  Pre event speaker amplification

Speaker social kits currently go out only after the webinar. The speaker's audience is the target audience, and asking after the event converts nobody. One extra asset in the same creative batch is the cheapest registration lift available.

3.6  Partner list co promotion

Partners are described as having large relevant audiences, and the reason for running with them is to reach new names. Nobody has ever asked a partner for their list size or agreed a reciprocal send. The same question is open and unasked for the next two partner sessions.

3.7  The personalised send

A personalised email is reported to have produced a 60 percent increase in signups on one webinar. It is the most promising tactic on record, and it has not been repeated.

HANDLE CAREFULLY  That figure is second hand, relayed rather than pulled, with no baseline, denominator or date. It is on the reconstruction list in 6.7. Verify it in HubSpot before it is quoted upward. A document whose central charge is that nobody measures the funnel cannot rest its best evidence on an unconfirmed number.

3.8  Paid and sponsored webinars, now a decision rather than a build

There is about $175,000 to $200,000 of paid media left for the year, and the current position is that webinars are probably not the right place for it. That position is explicitly provisional and the evidence that would change it has been named: the recent history of paid advertising around webinars, and the quality of the leads it produced.

Four steps answer it.

  1. Pull the recent paid history from the demand generation calendar that holds it.
  2. Score the leads it produced against the target account list.
  3. Pair the result with the placement finding in 2.6, which explains poor leads without blaming the channel.
  4. Bring a verdict before the September spend decision, not an opinion.

The reason for the doubt is the organic target in 3.10, not the tactic. A paid case that arrives before the organic baseline exists will lose. The original argument stands if the evidence supports it: sponsored placements at $12,000 to $15,000 each generate new names, allow a real cost per lead to be calculated, and the vendor supplies speakers, which solves a real sourcing problem.

3.9  A target account attendance flag

The request is simple: when someone from a named target account registers or attends, flag it to the account owner. That is a HubSpot workflow combining target list membership, a form submission and a representative notification. It belongs to whoever owns HubSpot workflows.

3.10  The organic path to 100 to 400 registrations

The programme now has a registration target: 100 to 400 people per webinar, or more, reached organically through targeted email, brand social, the brand handle, and executive social posting. Four channels. Three have owners.

THE HONEST PROBLEM  No webinar in the record has a registration figure, so nobody can say whether 100 to 400 is a stretch or a floor. That converts the funnel baseline in 6.7 from useful to urgent.

3.11  What is not measured is itself an opportunity

  • No cost per lead for any webinar, ever.
  • No show rate tracked as a series. The only datapoint is a hedged recollection of one session, roughly 75 registered and 15 attended, about a webinar on a platform that has since been dropped.
  • No replay to meeting path.
  • No comparison between formats, despite three format changes in eight months.

5.6  The gating rule

Three data points exist and they do not add up to a decision. The replay was ruled ungated in one meeting. Gating was proposed in a second and drew no comment either way. In a third, gating was mentioned inside a list of ten items and the whole list was agreed. That is a blanket agreement, not a ruling: stronger than silence and weaker than a decision. The rule below should go into the document so that anyone who disagrees is disagreeing with something specific.

AssetGatedWhy
Live registrationYes, HubSpot formIt is the only reliable identity capture in the whole programme
Asset bundle: deck, one page summary, transcriptYesHigh intent, low friction, and it re identifies replay viewers
The replay recordingNoAlready ruled, and Vidyard resolves known contacts anyway
Clips and socialNoTop of funnel

WHAT UNGATING COSTS  Vidyard only resolves contacts already known to HubSpot, so cold paid social traffic to an ungated replay is invisible. The asset bundle is what replaces that capture, and it should be said in the same breath.

5.7  The value checklist, before a date is booked

A webinar that cannot answer these does not get a date.

  1. Who is this for? A named audience or target account segment, not a business unit.
  2. How many of them can we actually reach? A list size, from HubSpot, before the invite is written.
  3. What do we want them to do next? Book a meeting, download the bundle, join a follow up.
  4. Who receives the lead, and will they work it? A named person.
  5. What does the partner bring? List size, a reciprocal send, speaker amplification.
  6. What are the six assets we will get out of the recording, and does the video team have room?
  7. What is the success measure, agreed with the requester before the date is booked?
  8. Would we run this if there were no announcement attached to it? If not, it is an announcement rather than a webinar.
  9. Can this reach 100 to 400 registrations organically? If the reachable segment is two hundred people, say so before the date is booked.
  10. Who is the marketing manager on this account? They define the audience, the segment, the product and the geography.
  11. Which executives will post about it? Named, with a brief and a date.

6.2  The capture architecture

Traffic arrives from four sources: paid, organic social, the partner list, and past attendees. All of it lands on the landing page, and the HubSpot form is the identity capture.

The workflow then does five things in order. It sets the contact as a marketing contact. It stamps the three Salesforce guard fields described in 6.3. It sets the Salesforce campaign. It sends an instant confirmation carrying the joining link. It enrols the contact in the pre event reminder sequence.

After the session the audience splits in two. Attendees receive the replay and the asset bundle. Non attendees receive the replay and a short apology note. Both paths rejoin at Vidyard watch data, which writes back to the contact record through the tracking parameter. Fit score combined with engagement then decides the outcome: at or above threshold the contact becomes a marketing qualified lead in Salesforce and reaches an inbound representative, and below threshold it stays in nurture and is invited to the next webinar.

6.4  Scoring, and the threshold

A contact fit score already exists, built inside GTM Operations, with edit rights offered. The proposed threshold sends anything above 75 to Salesforce as a marketing qualified lead. Three things must be fixed before it routes anybody.

  1. The weights have never been checked by anyone. One review found a penalty of only eight points for an obviously disqualifying signal.
  2. It is capped by enrichment gaps. Seniority, business area and company category are empty on new contacts, which holds the score down. A brand new webinar registrant is exactly the population this breaks on.
  3. It has no engagement dimension. Fit describes who someone is. Webinars produce intent: attended, stayed, asked a question, watched the replay. There is also a concrete reason the engagement half does not exist, which is that webinar registration and attendance are not HubSpot properties, so the score cannot see them and the organisation is scoring on email opens instead.

THE PROPOSAL  Qualify on fit multiplied by engagement, not fit alone. A director at a tier one bank who registered and never appeared is not a lead. A vice president who attended, stayed to the end and downloaded the bundle is, even at a lower fit score. Making registration and attendance into properties is the build that turns this from an argument into a score.

6.5  Lead quality, and whether off profile registrants belong in the CRM

The open question is whether registrants who are not the right profile should be kept at all. The answer proposed here has three parts.

  • Yes to the CRM. Off profile registrants cost almost nothing to hold now that marketing contacts sit at about 261,000, down from about 360,000 and comfortably below the 360,000 to 370,000 warning band, and they are the audience for retail and community programmes.
  • No to sales. Only fit combined with engagement crosses the threshold.
  • They are a targeting signal rather than a verdict on the channel. Fix the targeting and the same channel produces different people.

6.6  The sales hand off

A qualified lead lands with junior salespeople inside GTM Operations, who qualify any inbound lead and route it onward by business line.

Design for capacity rather than volume. Per webinar, hand over the top five to ten leads with the evidence attached: what they attended, how long they stayed, what they downloaded, and what their account is.

Close the loop as well. The unit of proof is one named opportunity traceable to one webinar, which is exactly what a previous account based email campaign produced.

6.7  The first numbers to publish

These are reconstructable this week, without running a new webinar. They matter more now that a target exists and there is nothing to compare it against.

  1. The 31 July send: size, opens, clicks, and who was on it.
  2. The most recent live session: registrations, attendance, and the reported personalised email lift.
  3. The most recent registrant list, scored against the target account list.
  4. Show rate across every session for which data exists.
  5. Cost per registration on the paid spend that has already run.

Five separate meetings have now decided the value of this channel from assertion. Whoever brings the first credible funnel sets the terms of all five.

Appendix. House rules for every webinar send

  • Nothing sends on a thirteenth of the month.
  • Never copy a link from a document. Outbound URLs are wrapped and it breaks sends silently. Open the link and copy the clean address from the browser bar.
  • Test the first name merge token. It has previously resolved to nothing across a send of about four thousand contacts.
  • Test forms in a private window with an address that is not already in Salesforce. A recognised contact passes regardless and proves nothing.
  • Never bulk export the CRM to build a list. It triggers a security alert the same night.
  • Reminders go five minutes before, not one hour. Confirmations send instantly, not on a delay.
  • Content review and legal review are two separate gates. Content review comes from the Asana queue and turns around in hours. Legal review is a ticket and takes one to three days, and copy must be locked before it is submitted.
  • Quadruple check webinar assets: the date, the registration link, the joining link clicked personally, the call to action, the subject line length, and whether the landing page is actually live.
  • One canonical campaign value per campaign. Free text campaign names are why reporting breaks.
