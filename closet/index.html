Closet.

A redesign of a friend's sliding-door closet. Two rods for hanging clothes on the left, four shelves on the right, and a top shelf in the hidden space above the doors. Brackets on the studs hold everything, so there is no centre panel. 73-1/2 x 24 x 96 in inside.

02

Why it's built this way

The closet had one shelf and one rod at 64 in, and a lot of empty wall. It holds mostly clothes, and the sliding panels stay as they are.

  • One half at a time. The two sliding panels are 38 in wide and overlap in the middle, so only half the closet opens at once. Each half gets one job: hanging on the left, shelves on the right.
  • Double hang. Two rods, at about 82 and 40 in, double the hanging space. Each holds clothes up to 37 in long on the hanger.
  • Shelves for folded clothes. Four 14 in deep shelves, 16 in apart. The bottom one is for shoes.
  • The space above the doors. The doors stop at 81 in but the ceiling is at 96. A 12 in deep shelf at 84 in uses that space for light bins.
  • No centre panel. Shelf and rod brackets on two studs hold both rods and the top shelf. The upper ones carry the shelf and the rod together. The right shelves sit on L brackets.
  • Everything goes into studs. Loaded shelves and rods pull on the wall. Drywall anchors alone are not enough.
  • Room to fit. Each shelf is 1/4 in shorter than its space. Walls are rarely square, so the shelves drop in without a fight.
  • Fits in a CR-V. The store rips the sheet and cuts each strip at 48 in. The longest finished piece is 36-5/8 in.

09

Build steps

One short session at the club, then an afternoon at the closet.

At the club: the wood

01
Cut the shelves

Trim the strips to 14, 14 and 12 in on the table saw. Cut 4 right shelves at 35 in and 2 top shelf halves at 36-5/8 in. Check these against your own measurements first.

02
Edges and finish

Optional. Iron banding onto the front edges and trim it. Sand to 180, then paint or wipe on poly. Let it dry before the install.

At the closet: the install

03
Clear it out and mark

Lift the 2 sliding panels up and out of the track. Take out the old shelf and rod and fill the holes. Find and mark the studs, then mark 84 in for the top shelf and 64, 48, 32 and 16 in on the right with a level.

04
Shelf and rod brackets

2 on the stud nearest the middle and 2 on the stud nearest the left wall. Upper pair with the top arm at 84 in, lower pair with the hook centre at 40 in. 3 #10 x 2-1/2" screws each, plumb before the last one.

05
Top shelf brackets

3 L brackets with 8 in arms: 1 between the upper shelf and rod brackets and 2 on the right. Lay the level across all the arms before you tighten.

06
Top shelf

Set the 2 halves on the brackets with the joint on the centre bracket. Screw up through the bracket holes, then join the halves on top with the 2 mending plates.

07
Right side brackets

2 L brackets with 12 in arms under each shelf height, on 2 different studs. Start at 64 in and work down, checking each pair with the level.

08
Right shelves

Each shelf starts 1-1/2 in right of the centre stud, so the one at 32 in clears the lower bracket. Screw up through the bracket holes with #8 x 5/8" pan-head screws.

09
Rods

Cut each rod to about 37-1/2 in, from 1/2 in off the left wall to 1-1/4 in past the centre hook. File the cut end and set each rod in its 2 hooks.

10
Panels back on

Set the sliding panels back on the track and check they slide freely. Load heavy items low. The top shelf is for light bins only.

Before cutting

Find the stud nearest the middle of the back wall first. The shelf lengths and the top shelf joint depend on where it is.

Keep the club's shop rules: the riving knife on, the blade just above the wood, a push stick, and stand off to the side of the table saw.
