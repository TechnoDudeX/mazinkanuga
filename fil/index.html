4.2 — The email

From: Chainlink Labs

Subject: Fidelity International on what a tokenized fund needs to be useful

Preheader: A 30-minute conversation on tokenized liquidity, fund data, and what changes for custody, collateral, and treasury.

Fidelity International tried a tokenized fund that did not work. The second one does.

Two and a half years ago, Fidelity International issued a digital twin — a token that mirrored a traditional fund share while the share, and every process behind it, stayed exactly where it was. It got no distribution uptake and no secondary market activity.

In this recorded conversation, Emma Pecenicic, Head of Digital Asset Distribution at Fidelity International, tells Frank Seibold, Global Head of Banking & Capital Markets Sales at Chainlink Labs, what they changed and why.

The short version: tokenizing a share achieves nothing on its own. What makes the instrument useful is the data that travels with it — NAV, yield, distribution ratios — available on chain, to any system that needs it, without a manual file from the asset manager.

What you will hear:

  • Why digital twins stalled, and what “digital native” means in practice — subscriptions and redemptions on chain, and the token recognised as the share
  • How the Chainlink Runtime Environment (CRE) publishes NAV, yield to maturity, 30-day yield and distribution ratios on chain, consistently across multiple chains — including the process to republish a corrected NAV
  • What this changes for a collateral platform that needs to fetch a NAV, apply a haircut, and confirm eligibility without waiting for a file
  • Why Emma’s ambition is a single source of truth that removes reconciliation between the asset manager, the distributor, the administrator and the data vendors
  • Where she expects tokenized liquidity to go over the next 12 to 24 months

It runs 30 minutes. No slides.

05 · Non-negotiable

Six rules. Every one of them came out of the recording or out of a previous rejection.

  1. Rule one This is not an investment promotion. Do not name a yield figure, do not describe the Fidelity product as an investment, and do not use the word “returns”. The legal boundary is on the record: “we are not in the business of selling investment.”

  3. Rule three Do not name the JP Morgan chain in the email. The transcript renders it as “Kinesis”. The likely correct name is Kinexys. Confirm it, or leave it out.

07 · Wide net, then trim

How to build
this list

Build it large, look at the real job titles it caught, then cut the tokens that pulled the wrong people. Do not try to write the perfect filter first.

7.0 — Before you start, check the classification fields

Free-text job title is the fallback. Check first whether Josh’s classification fields can do the job better.

  1. Settings (gear icon) → Properties.
  2. Set the object filter to Contact.
  3. Search classification, then tier, then segment, then persona.
  4. For each field found, open it and read Field type and the values in the dropdown.
  5. If a field holds values such as asset manager, custodian or fund admin, use that field instead of job title in step 7.2 filter B. It will be cleaner and it will not miss non-English titles.

If the fields are empty or the values do not map, continue with job title.

7.1 — Build list one, the inclusion pool

HubSpot joins filters inside a group with AND, and joins separate groups with OR. One list-membership filter holds one list. So you cannot write “in list A or list B” in the same group as everything else. Build the OR as its own list first.

  1. Contacts → Lists → Create list (top right).
  2. Object: Contact-based. Type: Active list.
  3. Name it: POOL — BCM institutional inclusion.
  4. Add the first filter: List membership → is a member of → ALL - Institutional Investors - Primary Segment.
  5. Click Add filter group, not “Add filter”. In the new group, add List membership → is a member of → BCM 2025 (ICP).
  6. Repeat step 5, one group per list, for:
    • BCM ICP Opted-in
    • CM SFDC Tier Opted in
    • BCM subscription
    • TAG list
    • IR Distro — only if open item 4 is closed

The
trim

Every token below is a real risk. Read the titles the net actually caught before you trust the count.

  1. Open the saved list. The contact table appears.
  2. Click Actions → Edit columns. Add Job title and Company name. Remove the columns you do not need.
  3. Sort by Job title. Read three or four pages, from the top and from the middle.
  4. Look for the known traps in the table below.
