01 · Part 1, Q1

The main
objective

Lead generation into banking and capital markets, using thought leadership as the vehicle rather than the goal.

Three outcomes, in priority order:

  1. Net-new, targeted namesFor the European sales team — accounts and seniority we cannot reach through our own list. Our own webinars produce volume without precision.
  2. Brand adjacencyChainlink discussed on the same platform, and the same panel, as established European asset managers, custodians and administrators.
  3. A measurable result we can repeatThis is our first paid webinar placement of this kind and it is being run as a test with a stated success metric.
Not this

This is not a product launch and should not be shaped as one. No demo, no announcement dependency, no launch date to hit.

02 · Part 1, Q2

What to
highlight

No product pitch. The subject is the infrastructure layer underneath tokenised finance, illustrated through deployments that are already public.

Capability How it should appear
Cross-chain interoperability (CCIP)The answer to fragmentation — moving assets and messages between chains and into existing bank systems
Data StreamsThe price and market-data layer that on-chain funds and collateral depend on
Proof of ReserveVerifiable backing — the audit and transparency argument

Public deployments available as illustration: Franklin Templeton’s Benji fund (Chainlink component), Project Pangea, the DTCC appchain work, JP Morgan payments, and the Swift interoperability work.

⚠️ Constraint

Any client named on the day, or in the write-up, needs that client’s own approval first. Please assume nothing is usable until it is confirmed.

03 · Part 1, Q3

Target
audience

Primary — the “why” audience (Funds Europe)

COOs, heads of operations, heads of digital assets, heads of product and heads of transformation at asset managers, custodians, fund administrators and banks. Geography: UK, Luxembourg, Ireland, Switzerland and mainland Europe.

Secondary — the “how” audience (Funds Tech)

CTOs, heads of technology and heads of architecture at the same firms. Your own framing applies: the how goes into Funds Tech and the why goes into Funds Europe.

Welcome, not targeted

US head offices of European institutions. Decisions agreed in London are frequently signed off in New York or Chicago.

Out of scope

Retail investors, crypto-native and Web3-native audiences, and developer audiences. These are served elsewhere and should not shape the promotion.

Request

The audience breakdown by seniority, firm type and geography across Funds Europe, Funds Tech and the MENA/Asia titles, plus the registrant profile from the FundsTech Forum.

04 · Part 1, Q4

The five
messages

The top messages to convey, in order.

01

Tokenisation has moved from pilot to production, and the remaining constraint is operational, not conceptual.

The question has changed from whether to how it runs at scale, every day, inside existing controls.

02

The value is in the middle and back office, not in distribution alone.

Settlement, reconciliation, collateral mobility and lifecycle servicing are where the cost sits — and where tokenisation pays for itself first. Distribution is the visible part; operations is the profitable part.

03

A tokenised asset is only as good as the data and the connectivity underneath it.

Verifiable reserves, reliable valuation, and the ability to move between chains and into legacy systems are preconditions, not features. This is where infrastructure standards matter.

04

Interoperability is the precondition for liquidity.

Dozens of isolated chains and private ledgers reproduce exactly the fragmentation the industry is trying to escape. Connectivity is what turns tokenised assets into tradeable ones.

05

This is now happening inside regulated frameworks, not around them.

The FCA’s Digital Securities Sandbox, Luxembourg’s CSSF approvals for tokenised funds, and the EU framework mean the compliance path exists. The conversation is about execution.

05 · Part 1, Q5

Trends we
would comment on

Industry trends, regulations and challenges the spokesperson is comfortable taking on.

  • The settlement legInstant settlement in a tokenised world, and what happens to traditional liquidity provision when it moves to minutes. Stablecoins and tokenised cash as the cash leg.
  • Regulatory momentum in Europe and the UKMiCA, the Bank of England / FCA Digital Securities Sandbox, and Luxembourg CSSF approvals for tokenised UCITS.
  • Vendor and platform proliferationThe operational cost of too many third-party providers, and the pull toward consolidated, interoperable infrastructure.
  • Data sovereigntyAnd dependence on US-based infrastructure — a rising concern in European operations teams.
  • Where AI and tokenisation competeFor the same budget and the same transformation capacity.
  • Board ambition vs operational readinessThe gap between the two — the COO / CTO relationship as the real bottleneck.
06 · Part 1, Q6

Off limits

Areas the spokesperson does not want to cover.

  • Token price and tokenomicsLINK token price, tokenomics or market performance — or anything that reads as investment commentary. This is a firm line.
  • Unapproved namesAny client, partner or deal that is not already public — and even public ones only with that client’s approval for this specific use.
  • Competitor comparisonNamed competing protocols, chains or infrastructure vendors.
  • RoadmapUnannounced product roadmap or timelines.
  • Legal or compliance opinionWe describe how the technology works; we do not advise on whether a given structure is compliant in a given jurisdiction.
  • Anything unapprovedAny claim we have not had pre-approved — see Part 2, question 4 — this is the practical constraint on the whole piece.

10 · Part 2, Q4

What needs
pre-approval

Yes — and this drives the production schedule, so please build it into the timeline.

Pre-approval is required for:

  • Any named client, partner or institution
  • Any quantified claim — volumes, values, transaction counts, market share
  • Anything touching regulatory status, licensing or compliance
  • Any forward-looking statement about product or adoption
  • The spokesperson’s biography and title as published
The ask on timing

Please allow five working days for review of any copy, abstract, promotional text or write-up before publication, and share the promotional copy with us at the same time as the editorial copy.
