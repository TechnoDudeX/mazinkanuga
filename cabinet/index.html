02

Why it's built this way

It started as a copy of a YouTube microwave cart. Once my appliances were measured and I wanted doors and closed sides, it turned into a buffet cabinet. Then I walked Kent and Chad at the Durham Woodworking Club through it, and they talked me into a back, a pair of frames and paint-grade materials. This is the version after that conversation.

  • Frames first. Two poplar 1x3 frames, one at the top and one at the bottom, are built square before anything else. The plywood sides fasten to them, so the box goes together square.
  • A back in a groove. 1/4" hardboard slides into a groove near the back edge of each side. Nothing gets pushed out the back, and the box stays square.
  • Front rails stop the sag. A 1x2 on edge is glued to the front of the bottom and the shelf. It stiffens them and covers the plywood edges.
  • Short legs. Four 3.8 cm blocks leave 7 cm clear underneath. The back pair also holds the back in place.
  • Full overlay doors. Two plywood doors on soft-close cabinet hinges, drilled with a concealed hinge jig. No catches needed.
  • Painted black. To match the counters. That means paint-grade plywood is all it needs, and the top comes from the same plywood instead of glued-up hardwood.

Plywood sheet layout

Have the lumber yard rip the sheet into three strips about 1 cm oversize (45, 39 and 35 cm) so it's easy to carry. Their cuts aren't guaranteed exact, so trim the strips to 44, 38 and 34 cm in the shop, then crosscut. The top comes from a separate 2 x 4 ft panel.

Screws and tools

56 screws in 6 joints, and only 3 kinds. Each drawing is a section through one joint, to scale. Every joint gets glue too. In the 3D model, turn on Screws and tap one to zoom in on its joint.

  • Pocket screws, frame corners16x

    Kreg #8 x 1-1/4" pocket screws: coarse thread, washer head, square drive

    2 per corner, from the ends of the short pieces into the long pieces.

    Jig and stepped bit set for 3/4" stock. The hole goes in at 15° to the face, on the face that gets hidden. Drive with the 6" #2 square bit, snug, not hard.

    Goes about 1.3 cm into the long piece. Glue the corner too.

  • Screws, bottom panel into its frame6x

    #8 x 1-1/4" flat-head wood screws, square drive

    Straight down through the panel into the long frame pieces, 3 front and 3 back.

    About 1.4 cm into the frame, 0.5 cm short of its underside. Glue too.

    About 1.3 cm into the 1.8 cm side, so about 0.5 cm short of the outside. Glue too.

  • Pocket screws, shelf into the sides8x

  • Screws into the legs8x

    #8 x 2-1/2" flat-head wood screws, square drive

    About 1.3 cm into the top, 0.5 cm short of its surface. Glue too.

The other screws

  • Hinges: 16 #6 x 5/8" pan-head screws, 2 in each cup and 2 in each plate. Plenty of hinge packs include them.
  • Knobs: 2 8-32 x 1" machine screws, which come with the knobs. Drill a 3/16" hole through the door for each.
  • Wall strap: the anti-tip kit's screws, one bracket into the top frame and one into a wall stud.

09

Build steps

Two shop sessions, then finishing at home.

Session 1: cut and prep

01
Rough-cut the sheet at the yard

Three strips along the length, about 45, 39 and 35 cm wide.

02
Trim to size

Rip the strips to 44, 38 and 34 cm, then crosscut the doors (82), sides (83), bottom and shelf (85). Top 94 x 42 from the 2 x 4 panel, back 86 x 83 from the hardboard. Check every part with a machinist square.

03
Groove the sides

Mark the back edges as a mirror pair first. Then a groove 0.6 in from the back edge and 0.6 deep, the full length, as wide as the hardboard is thick (test on an offcut). Both grooves at the back, facing in.

04
Cut the poplar

All 4 long frame pieces (85) together against a stop block, then all 4 short ones (22), then 2 rails (85) and 2 leg blanks (35).

05
Glue-ups and edges

Glue the leg blanks face to face, then cut 4 legs at 7 cm from the clamped blank. Edge band the doors, the side fronts and the top's front and side edges.

Session 2: build it

06
Build the frames

Six pocket holes in each short piece, all on one face (jig and bit set for 3/4" stock, 15°): 2 in each end for the corners, 2 in the outside edge for the sides later. Glue and drive 16 Kreg #8 x 1-1/4" coarse pocket screws into the corners, then check the diagonals.

07
Front rails

Glue a rail to the front of the bottom panel and the shelf, flush with the top and both ends, and clamp. No screws needed. Then 4 pocket holes in each end of the shelf, underneath.

08
Bottom panel onto its frame

Frame pockets facing down. Glue the panel on so the rail covers the front of the frame, then 6 #8 x 1-1/4" flat-head screws down into the frame, countersunk just below the surface.

09
Put the box together

Sides up, grooves at the back. Glue the bottom assembly in and drive 2 pocket screws from underneath through each short piece into the side. Then the top frame, pockets up, the same way. Check the diagonals.

10
Fix the shelf

Two 39 cm spacers on the bottom panel, behind the front rails, a ledger block clamped under each end so the pocket screws can't pull it out of line, then glue and drive 4 pocket screws into each side.

11
Slide the back in

Cabinet on its front, hardboard up into the grooves from underneath.

12
Legs

Glue a block under each corner, front pair 2 cm back behind the rail. 2 #8 x 2-1/2" flat-head screws down into each from inside, about 1 cm in from the side: pre-drill 1/8" about 6 cm deep and countersink. The back legs hold the back in.

13
Top

Upside down on the top, flush at the back and centred. Glue, then 10 #8 x 1-1/4" flat-head screws up through the top frame, heads flush and no deeper.

14
Hang the doors

Hinge cups 10 cm from the door ends, plates 10.5 cm from the top and bottom of each side, all with #6 x 5/8" pan-head screws. Set the middle gap with the hinges' side screws: about 3 mm, and anything from 2 to 5 mm looks fine.

At home

15
Fill and paint

Doors and hardware off. Fill the screw holes and edges, sand, prime, then 2 coats of black. Knobs 6 cm down, 4 cm in: a 3/16" hole and the 8-32 screw that comes with each.

16
Strap it to the wall

The anti-tip kit: one bracket into the top frame, one into a stud, before anything goes on or in it.

Shop notes from Kent and Chad

Cut matching pieces together against a stop block. Start the miter saw before the wood touches the blade and let it stop before lifting. On the table saw, keep the riving knife on, the blade just above the wood, and stand off to the side.

Check your squares; the shop's wall squares aren't all square. And if you're rushed, distracted or tired, leave it for another day.
