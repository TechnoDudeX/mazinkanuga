Console.

A solid-wood console table with splayed legs and a low shelf for baskets, stained dark. Built from construction lumber: a glued 2x6 top on a 2x4 base. 40 in long, 10 in deep and 32 in tall, sized with my wife for the spot it goes in.

02

Why it's built this way

It started from two photos of a dark wood console with leaning legs and baskets underneath. The goal was the same look from lumber any big box store sells, with joints a first-year club member can cut well.

  • Legs lean one way only. They splay out along the length at 10°, not front to back. Every leg is the same part, cut with two parallel miter cuts.
  • Cleats carry the top. A 2x4 laid flat under each end. The legs pocket-screw up into it, and the top screws down onto it.
  • The top can move. Solid wood gets wider and narrower with the seasons. The outer screw holes in each cleat are slotted, and the top has no glue.
  • The shelf locks it square. End stretchers and two long rails tie the four legs together, so the base can't rack.
  • Construction lumber, done right. Ripping the 2x4s to 2-1/2 in and the 2x6s to 5 in takes off the round edges, so the joints and glue lines are crisp.
  • Stained dark. Conditioner first, because pine takes dark stain in blotches. Then wipe-on poly.

What goes on the shelf

At 10 in deep, a plain shelf between the legs would only be 5-1/2 in deep. So the shelf runs out to the outside of the legs, 8-1/2 in deep, with a small notch at each corner. The legs lean in as they go up, so keep baskets inside them.

Board layout

Each cut has about 1 in extra to trim. On the third 2x4, crosscut the cleats off first while the board is full width, then rip the rest to 2-1/2 in.

Screws and tools

38 screws in 5 joints, and only 3 kinds. Each drawing is a section through one joint, to scale. Every joint gets glue except the top. In the 3D model, turn on Screws and tap one to zoom in on its joint.

  • Pocket screws, legs into the cleats8x

    Kreg 2-1/2" pocket screws: coarse thread, washer head, square drive

    2 per leg, from the inside face near the top, up into the cleat.

    Jig and stepped bit set for 1-1/2" stock. The hole goes in at 15° on the inside face of the leg, the face that looks at the other leg. Drive with the 6" #2 square bit, snug, not hard.

    Goes about 1" into the 1-1/2" cleat. Glue too.

  • Pocket screws, stretchers into the legs8x

    Kreg 2-1/2" pocket screws: coarse thread, washer head, square drive

    2 per end, from the stretcher's face that looks at the middle of the table.

    Jig and stepped bit set for 1-1/2" stock. A scrap block under the stretcher holds it at height while you drive.

    About 1" into the leg. Glue too.

  • Pocket screws, rails into the stretchers8x

    Kreg 2-1/2" pocket screws: coarse thread, washer head, square drive

    2 per end, from the inside face of each rail.

    Jig and stepped bit set for 1-1/2" stock. Tops flush with the stretchers.

    About 1" into the stretcher. Glue too.

  • Screws, shelf onto the frame8x

    #8 x 2" flat-head wood screws, square drive

    Straight down through the shelf: 2 into each stretcher, 2 into each rail.

    Pre-drill with a #8 countersink bit (1/8" pilot), set the heads just below the surface, then fill. The baskets hide them.

    About 1-1/4" into the rail or stretcher.

  • Screws, cleats into the top6x

    #10 x 2-1/2" washer-head or pan-head screws

    Straight up through the cleats into the top, 3 per cleat.

    Middle hole 3/16", round. Outer 2 holes 3/16" wide and slotted 1/2" long front to back, so the top can move. No glue.

    About 1" into the top, 1/2" short of its surface.

09

Build steps

Two shop sessions, then finishing at home.

Before: pick and rest the wood

01
Pick the boards

Straight boards with few knots. Let them sit inside at home for a week before cutting; construction lumber is often still wet and moves as it dries.

Session 1: the top and the cuts

02
Glue up the top

Rip about 1/4" off each edge of both top boards cut from the 2x6, so each is 5" wide. Glue edge to edge and clamp every 10", clamps over and under. Leave it overnight.

03
Rip the 2x4s

Crosscut the cleats (9-1/2") off board 3 first. Then rip everything else to 2-1/2" wide.

04
Cut the legs

Miter saw at 10°. Cut one end, slide the board along without flipping it, and cut the other end at the same setting. Stop block at 29-7/16" on the long edge, then all 4 legs.

05
Cut the rest

Stretchers (5-1/2") and the shelf (rip the 1x10 to 8-1/2"). Cut the rails and shelf a little long; trim them after the ends are built.

Session 2: build it

06
Drill the pocket holes

Jig set for 1-1/2" stock. 2 near the top of each leg on its inside face, 2 in each end of the stretchers (face toward the middle) and of the rails (inside face).

07
Build the two ends

Cleat upside down on the bench. Legs 1/2" in from each cleat end, centred on its width, leaning out. Glue and screw. Then the stretcher, its top 7-1/4" from the floor, on a 4-3/4" scrap block.

08
Join the ends

Trim the rails to fit between the stretchers. Glue and screw them, tops flush. Measure the diagonals and push the base until they match.

09
Shelf

Trim it so its ends line up with the outside of the stretchers. Mark a 1-1/2 x 2-1/2" notch at each corner from the base itself and cut them with a jigsaw, so the shelf fits around the legs. Then 8 #8 x 2" screws down into the rails and stretchers, countersunk, then fill.

10
Flatten and trim the top

Planer or sander until it's flat, then cut it to 40 x 10". Drill the cleat holes: middle round, outer 2 slotted 1/2" front to back.

At home

11
Sand and stain

Sand to 180, then 220, and ease the edges. Conditioner, then stain (test on an offcut first), then 3 coats of wipe-on poly. Do the underside of the top too, so it stays flat.

12
Attach the top

Top face down on a blanket, base upside down on it, 5" from each end to the cleats. 6 #10 x 2-1/2" screws up through the cleats. No glue. Felt pads on the feet.

Before cutting

Walk Kent and Chad through the leg-to-cleat joint. Pocket screws and glue hold it, but a half-lap or two dowels there is stronger and good practice.

Keep the club's shop rules: the riving knife on, the blade just above the wood, a push stick, and stand off to the side of the table saw.
